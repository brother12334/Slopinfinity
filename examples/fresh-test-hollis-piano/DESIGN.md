# Fresh test: Hollis Piano Tuning

A new kind of site for the skill (a one-person home service), built without style coaching.

## Brief

Hollis Piano Tuning, a one-person piano tuning service.
- Standard tuning: $140, about 90 minutes.
- Pitch raise for pianos not tuned in more than 2 years: adds $60 and about 30 minutes.
- Home visits Monday to Friday, 9 am to 5 pm, within 20 miles of Elm Grove.
- Booking is by request form; the tuner replies by email to confirm a time. On the demo the
  form is not connected.
- No reviews, photos, credentials or tuner name supplied. Don't invent any.

Visitor's decision: is it worth booking, what will it cost, am I in range? Primary action:
Request a tuning.

## Process

- **Content check:** price, time, area and hours support the decision. Trust (who the tuner
  is, experience, reviews) is missing and recorded as unresolved, not filled.
- **First questions (answered by assumption for the test):** photos of the tuner or work?
  (none: text-led); is the form connected? (no: demo check only); tuner's name and
  experience? (not shown).
- **Asset decision:** ladder rung 4, an intentional text-led page. Wikimedia Commons was
  still rate-limiting this machine, and a stock photo of a piano would add atmosphere, not
  evidence. The one graphic is a cost estimator built only from the supplied prices. The
  key strip at the top is the single decorative element, taken from the subject.
- **Real sites studied:** charity: water (the amount control sits in the hero), GOV.UK
  (plain task, predictable confirmation), NYC311 (organized by what the visitor does).
- **Type and color (chosen last):** Literata for headings, Work Sans for text; ebony,
  white and a felt red taken from piano hammer felt, for the one action color.

## Problems found after rendering, and revisions

1. Price rows ran the duration into the name ("Standard tuningAbout 90 minutes"). Fixed.
2. The key strip's black keys were spaced irregularly. Rebuilt in the real 2-and-3 pattern.
3. Mobile: the primary action sat below the first screen. Tightened the hero and added a
   compact "Request" button in the header.

## What the visitor can and cannot do

| Can | Cannot yet |
|---|---|
| See the price for their piano ($140, $200, or both if unsure) and how long it takes | Know who the tuner is, their experience or training |
| See the hours and the service area | Read reviews or see past work |
| Fill in and check a request; the estimate carries into the form | Actually send a request, or get a confirmed time (form not connected) |
| | Check precisely whether their address is within 20 miles (the page asks them to say if unsure) |

## Unresolved content needs

The tuner's name, a photo, experience or qualifications, and any real reviews; a connected
request form with the email confirmation the brief describes.
