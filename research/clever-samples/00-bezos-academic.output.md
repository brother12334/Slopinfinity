Jeff Bezos is often described as starting an online book store in his garage in Bellevue, Washington in 1994 after leaving a high level job at D.E. Shaw, a hedge fund (Stone, 2013). While this is technically true, it omits key details. Bezos was born in Albuquerque in 1964 and had trained in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at Princeton. This was a strategic risk, as he knew the web would be growing rapidly in the near future. While retail was important, arguably the most impactful thing he has done is create Amazon Web Services. They created the infrastructure to allow companies to rent their computing resources. They were originally created for the internal use of Amazon, but by 2006 they were launched. Within a decade they were being used by companies as big as Netflix. This is where Amazon can now be seen as more of an infrastructure company.

Bezos started his own aerospace company called Blue Origin in 2000. He also purchased The Washington Post in 2013 for $250 million. In July 2021, around 2 weeks after handing over the role of Chief Executive to Andy Jassy, Bezos flew to the edge of space in the Blue Origin New Shepard Capsule. There is mixed opinions about how successful his achievements are. From a customer perspective he has made things cheaper and easier. However, journalists and labour researchers have highlighted how poor the working conditions are in the warehouses. This is seen as the trade-off for making things cheaper. Khan (2017) argued that the traditional anti-trust laws were poorly suited to recognising the market power Amazon had gained due to their focus on keeping consumer prices low. This is less to do with Bezos and more to do with what we expect very large businesses to be responsible for.
