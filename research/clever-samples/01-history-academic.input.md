The printing press, developed by Johannes Gutenberg around 1440, fundamentally transformed the circulation of knowledge in early modern Europe. Before its introduction, books were copied by hand, a slow and expensive process that confined literacy largely to the clergy and the wealthy elite. Movable type dramatically reduced the cost of producing texts, and within fifty years an estimated twenty million volumes had been printed across the continent. This proliferation of printed material played a pivotal role in the Protestant Reformation, as Martin Luther's writings spread far more rapidly than church authorities could suppress them. Moreover, the standardization of texts facilitated the development of modern science, allowing scholars in different countries to work from identical copies of the same data. Ultimately, the printing press laid the groundwork for the information age, demonstrating how a single technological innovation can reshape the social, religious, and intellectual landscape of an entire civilization.
