The printing press was invented by Johannes Gutenberg in around 1440 which had a huge impact on the sharing of knowledge during early modern Europe. Before the invention, books had to be written by hand meaning there was a long and costly process to create books. However, the movable type made it much cheaper to produce text and 20 million books were printed across the continent within 50 years. The increase in printed books was extremely important for the Protestant Reformation as Martin Luther's writings were shared much quicker than they could be censored by the church. The creation of uniform texts meant that modern science could develop as scientists across the world were working off the same copies of data. Overall the printing press led to the information age, showing how one piece of technology can change the whole society, religion and knowledge of an entire civilisation.
