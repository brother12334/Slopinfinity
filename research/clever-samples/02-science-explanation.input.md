Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy. It takes place primarily in the chloroplasts, specialized organelles containing the green pigment chlorophyll, which absorbs light most efficiently in the blue and red wavelengths. During the light-dependent reactions, water molecules are split, releasing oxygen as a byproduct and generating the energy carriers ATP and NADPH. These molecules then power the Calvin cycle, in which carbon dioxide from the atmosphere is fixed into glucose. This process is not only essential for plant growth but also forms the foundation of nearly every food chain on Earth. Furthermore, photosynthesis is responsible for producing the oxygen that most living organisms depend on, making it one of the most important biological processes on the planet.
