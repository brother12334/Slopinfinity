Schools should start later in the morning for teenagers. Research consistently shows that adolescents experience a natural shift in their circadian rhythms, making it biologically difficult for them to fall asleep before 11 p.m. When classes begin at 7:30 a.m., many students are chronically sleep-deprived, which negatively affects their concentration, mood, and academic performance. Districts that have moved start times to 8:30 or later have reported improvements in attendance, grades, and even reductions in car accidents among teen drivers. Critics argue that later start times create scheduling challenges for transportation and extracurricular activities. However, these logistical concerns, while valid, should not outweigh the clear benefits to student health and learning. By aligning school schedules with adolescent biology, communities can invest in the well-being and success of the next generation.
