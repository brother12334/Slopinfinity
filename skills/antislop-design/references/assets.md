# Assets: use real images without getting stuck

Source: [five-site-review.pdf](five-site-review.pdf), pages 9 and 10. A photo is useful
only when it fits the subject, is usable, and is honest about what it depicts.

## The ladder

Choose per project, starting at the top, and record the choice in `DESIGN.md`.

1. **User-provided real material.** Photos of the actual place, product, person or work.
   The best source of identity. Ask for it when it would change the design.
2. **Verified licensed imagery.** From an allowed source with a clear license (for example
   Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash, Pexels). Verify the image page, creator, license and
   whether attribution is required. Save a local copy and a source record (title, creator,
   license, license URL, page URL). Credit it on the page.
3. **Original illustration or a concept render.** Drawn for the project. It must carry the
   same conviction, scale and detail as a photo-led design would.
4. **An intentional text-led design.** Type, structure and the task carry the page, as on
   GOV.UK or Stripe Docs. This is a design choice, not an absence.

## Labeling

- For a fictional café, library or venue, an unrelated photo may illustrate atmosphere or
  activity. Label it illustrative and never present it as the actual place.
- A shop needs true product imagery or clearly labeled concept renders. A photo of a real
  product presented as the fictional one is not allowed.
- Sample content (articles, people, rooms, tasks) is labeled once where it appears, and
  authored only with approval when it stands in for something substantial.

## Stop on download failure

Try **one** alternative permitted source. If access or licensing is still unclear, stop
retrying. Do not spend the task on network workarounds for one image. Ask the user to
upload assets, or move down the ladder, and recompose the page so no empty photo slot
remains. A designed fallback is part of the workflow, not a failure.

## Design the image, not just add it

Choose crop, scale, placement, caption and mobile treatment. A small generic rectangle,
photo or drawing, cannot supply identity by itself. On mobile, decide whether the image
leads, follows the task, or is cropped to a strip.
